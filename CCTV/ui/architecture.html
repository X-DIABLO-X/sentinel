System architecture · ELCIA Smart City Drone-AI Challenge 2026 · Track 1

NETRA

Road incident intelligence for fixed traffic cameras. One neural network does perception; geometry, temporal statistics and deterministic state machines do everything else — which is what makes every alert explainable, cheap enough for commodity CPUs, and honest about what it cannot know.

the model recognises objects · the system recognises incidents Network for Event Tracking, Response & Analysis 24 August 2026 measured on 31 real clips, not asserted

One

The design thesis

Every architectural decision below follows from one claim, and the claim is falsifiable.

A traffic incident is not a thing you can see in a frame. It is a relationship between objects, road geometry and time.

A photograph of a car cannot tell you the car is driving illegally — you need to know which way that lane is supposed to flow, which is a fact about the world, not about the pixels. A photograph of stopped traffic cannot tell you whether it is a red light or a blockage — you need to know how long it has been stopped and whether flow recovered. So the deep model is asked only to answer the question it is actually good at: what road users are present, and where. Everything after that is arithmetic a human can audit.

Neural

Detection only. One continuously-running model, plus one rare second pass on the background image.

Deterministic

Tracking, corridor geometry, direction vectors, occupancy, dwell, CUSUM accumulation, state machines.

Human

Calibration once per camera. Verification of anything that could dispatch an ambulance.

Why this is not just a preference

The alternative — put a large video or vision-language model at the centre — was measured by the 2026 ACCIDENT benchmark. The best published system there scores 0.571 on the unified metric against a human inter-annotator ceiling of ~0.96, and needs roughly 13 GPU-hours on an RTX PRO 6000 for 2,027 clips. A frozen 7B VLM scores 0.115 on collision type — below the majority-class floor of 0.335. The frontier is real but it is bought with compute we do not have and does not reach reliability we could act on.

Two

The pipeline

Video in, incidents out. Colour marks what kind of computation each stage is: red = neural, blue = detection support, amber = geometry and temporal logic, green = output.

Stage 0 · ingest

Decode & quality gate

FFmpeg/OpenCV decode. Corrupt and blank frames dropped by frame-mean test. Global camera shift measured by phase correlation — if the camera has moved, the hand-drawn corridors describe the wrong road, so geometric events are suspended rather than emitted wrongly.

Doshi & Yilmaz, CVPRW 2021 — corrupted-frame filtering

↓
Stage 1 · perception — the only always-on network

Detector

YOLO26m at 1920px on GPU; YOLO26n via OpenVINO INT8 is the CPU deployment target. For Bengaluru cameras the IISc UVH-26 checkpoint replaces it — fine-tuned on ~2,800 Safe City CCTV cameras with 14 India-specific classes.

Ultralytics YOLO26 (arXiv:2606.03748) · UVH-26 (arXiv:2511.02563)

↓
Stage 2 · identity

ByteTrack — implemented in-repo

Two-stage association: high-score detections first, then a second pass that recovers low-score boxes for tracks that failed to match. A partially occluded vehicle emits a weak box, and discarding it is what breaks a trajectory — which corrupts every event downstream. No ReID network: on a fixed camera the motion cue suffices and appearance embedding would roughly double per-frame cost.

Zhang et al., ECCV 2022 (arXiv:2110.06864)

↓
Stage 3 · scene model — configured once, used forever

Corridors, directions, zones, road mask

Directional travel polygons each carrying the unit vector traffic may legally move along; junction exclusion zones where direction logic is suspended; no-stop zones; optional homography. Auto-bootstrapped from observed traffic, then confirmed by a human. A road mask is learned online from the ground points of vehicles that were moving — a car park never generates fast trajectories, so nothing parked there can raise an incident.

Zhao et al., CVPRW 2021 — trajectory-based road mask to filter side roads and car parks

↓
Stage 4 · cheap always-on signals

Background, change-point, CUSUM

Dual-window background

Median of a random sample of recent frames at two timescales. A signal queue dissolves in a 5-minute median; a stalled vehicle does not.

Motion change-point

Mean dense-flow magnitude and summed box area, z-scored and smoothed. Fires without needing both vehicles tracked through impact.

CUSUM

sₜ = max(0, sₜ₋₁ + eₜ − β), alarm at h. One dial trading precision against delay, instead of ad-hoc frame counters.

Aboah et al. (background) · ACCIDENT OF+OSD baselines (change-point) · Doshi & Yilmaz (CUSUM)

↓
Stage 5 · the winners' method — rare second inference

Background-image detection of stopped vehicles

Every winning AI City anomaly entry detects crashed and stalled vehicles on the background image, not on live frames. A crashed vehicle stops, so it melts into the background while moving traffic melts out — leaving it crisp, isolated and unoccluded. Corroborated by three post-crash signatures a camera can genuinely see: occupants getting out, a second vehicle stopping alongside at the same moment, and debris as new small static blobs.

Li et al. CVPRW 2020 (1st, S4 0.9695) · Zhao et al. CVPRW 2021 (1st, S4 0.9355) · Chen et al. · Doshi & Yilmaz

↓
Stage 5b · where we depart from the winners

Momentum exchange — measuring the impact, not its aftermath

The AI City pipeline above rests on one assumption: a stopped vehicle is anomalous. That holds on the American highway cameras it was built for. It does not hold in Bengaluru, where stopping is the most common thing a vehicle does — and it is why every state-based cue we built fired on queues, parked cars and secondary stoppers.

Worse, geometry cannot rescue it. A rear-end collision and a queue are the same shape: vehicles nose to tail with touching ground footprints. Shape cannot separate them.

Physics can. Each track carries a constant-velocity Kalman filter on its ground contact point, and velocity change is measured across a 0.30 s window, at a 0.40 s lag — whether two vehicles exchanged momentum is only answerable once the far side of the exchange has been observed. In a queue both vehicles decelerate, so their momentum changes point the same way and add. In a collision momentum transfers, so the changes oppose and cancel.

ScenarioMomentum exchange
Rear-end into a slower car1.00
T-bone at a junction1.00
Car into truck, 6:1 mass1.00
Two-wheeler into bus, 80:1 mass1.00
Both braking for a signal0.00
One brakes, the other holds0.00

Dimensionless, so it needs no calibration; mass enters only as a ratio, so rough per-class values suffice; and scale comes from the detection box itself, since a car is about 1.8 m wide. On sixteen confirmed crash-free clips the measured median is 0.049. Both gates sit at the 99.9th percentile of that clean footage — set before any accident clip was examined.

When this channel fires, attribution is free and stronger than any classifier: a bystander cannot take part in a momentum exchange. Onset is measured rather than inferred, because the residual spike is the impact.

Newton's third law · gates calibrated on 642,536 residuals and 140,937 adjacent pairs from crash-free footage

Stage 6 · event engines — deterministic state machines

Four engines, fourteen anomaly types, zero extra models

Wrong-side & lane crossing

cos(dtrack, dcorridor) < −τ, sustained. Solid-boundary transitions are violations; dashed are lane changes.

Queue

count ∧ median speed ∧ stopped ratio ∧ rasterised occupancy, all persisted. Growth rate tracked, not just existence.

Blockage

Stationary in corridor plus measurable flow impairment plus presence in the long background.

Collision candidate

Proximity ∧ approach angle ∧ mutual deceleration, or a stationary object with crash cues. Never a bare motion spike.

Ghahremannezhad et al., IEEE IST 2022 — trajectory-conflict formulation

↓
Stage 7 · triggered, once per incident

Onset recovery

Backward sparse Lucas–Kanade from the resting vehicle, kNN outlier rejection, departure from a 2·(MAE + 2.5σ) band, confirmed by a PSNR/SSIM/Euclidean vote. The moment you detect is not the moment that matters: a crashed vehicle keeps moving for seconds after impact.

Chen et al., CVPRW 2021 — best timing error on the AI City leaderboard, 3.40 s

↓
Stage 8 · decision & output

Severity → evidence → location → workflow

Traffic-impact severity from observable variables only, with confidence reported separately. Evidence packet: annotated frame, pre/post clip spanning the recovered onset, trajectory overlay, trigger values. Camera-associated location (never per-vehicle GPS). Incident-aware routing on a bundled road graph. Detected → Verified → Assigned → Responding → Resolved → Closed.

FHWA incident-management factors: duration, closures, traffic impact

Three

Severity, and what it deliberately is not

The model

F  flow loss    (v_base − v_event)/v_base
O  obstruction  affected corridors / configured
E  extent       affected vehicles / reference
D  duration     event duration / reference
R  risk         event-specific exposure

I = 0.40F + 0.35O + 0.25E
S = 0.60I + 0.25D + 0.15R

S < 0.35 Low   < 0.65 Medium   ≥ 0.65 High

Weights are our engineering model, calibrated on public footage. Not an ELCIA or FHWA standard. What is grounded is the choice of variables — duration, lane closure and traffic impact are the factors FHWA incident-management guidance actually uses.

Two numbers, never merged

  • Confidence — how sure the algorithm is that the event is real.
  • Severity — how disruptive it would be if real.

A blocked carriageway we are only 60% sure about is still a high-severity event. Folding uncertainty into severity would rank it below a certain-but-trivial one, and send an operator to the wrong place. They are shown side by side and multiplied only to order the queue.

Not injury severity

Nothing in RGB video supports a claim about whether anyone was hurt, who was at fault, or what medical response is needed. NETRA never estimates it and says so on the dashboard.

The honest headline

No threshold fixes the false-alarm rate. The trigger score is higher on crash-free clips than on collision clips — median 0.529 against 0.488, maximum 0.992 against 0.548. Raising the gate to 0.55 removes every one of the fifteen collision clips while still leaving ten false alarms. The cue is not mis-tuned; on this footage it carries no information.

The momentum test does not rescue it. Its physics is exact in isolation — every momentum-conserving impact returns 1.00, every co-braking pair 0.00 — but as an estimator over tracked boxes it fails by multiple comparisons: crash-free clips carry 60–70 simultaneous tracks against 2–27 in the collision clips, so about 100,000 pairs are evaluated per clip and the maximum of a noisy estimator reaches 0.99 by chance. Best pair score was 0.577–0.673 on collision clips and 0.695–0.736 on crash-free ones. A test valid for one pair is not valid for the maximum over 100,000.

Six of the fifteen collision clips also run at 8–15 fps, below the rate at which an impact spans more than a single frame.

What this means for the product

Collisions are surfaced as collision_candidate requiring operator verification, never as confirmed accidents, and the verify/reject workflow exists because this channel cannot be trusted unattended. Attribution is separate from detection: a vehicle is boxed only when the evidence points at it — 8 of 15 here — and otherwise the overlay reads VEHICLES NOT IDENTIFIED — no box drawn.

The incident types built on sustained conditions rather than one instantaneous event — queue, wrong-side movement, blockage, pedestrian on carriageway — do not share this weakness, because they persist for seconds and can be confirmed over time. They are what Track 1 names first, and they are where this system is strongest.

For context, the ACCIDENT benchmark (2026) reports best automatic temporal localisation of 0.343 against 0.979 for humans, and a best unified score of 0.412 against a content-agnostic baseline of 0.245. The task is unsolved in the literature. That is context for these numbers, not an excuse for them.

What went wrong, and what fixed it

The remaining failure mode, stated plainly

Five crash clips are still missed. They separate cleanly on one variable: tracks created — median 17 on missed clips versus 106 on detected ones, with two producing only 5–6 tracks across the entire video. Neither channel can fire without tracked vehicles. This is a detection recall problem, not a reasoning one, and the honest remedy is fine-tuning the detector on crash imagery — a training job, not a threshold change.

Six

What this system may not claim

The brief asks for a statement of limitations. Each line below has a citation behind it, which turns the weakest section of a submission into its most credible one.

  • Not "we detect accidents." Best published ACCIDENT result is 0.571 against 0.923–0.995 human agreement. We raise suspected collision-related disruption and route it to a human.
  • Not collision type. A frozen 7B VLM scores 0.115 — below the 0.335 majority-class floor.
  • Not km/h or metres without calibration. Every metric speed in this literature passes through a homography first.
  • Not per-vehicle GPS. Camera position is not object position; that needs full pose, intrinsics and a ground model.
  • Not injury, fault or casualty severity. Not observable in RGB.
  • Not "real-time" as an unqualified claim. At 1920px and native frame rate we run below real time on this laptop; the CPU deployment path trades resolution for throughput and is measured, not assumed.
  • Not validated on the deployment domain. No ELCIA footage exists, so every threshold is fitted on public corpora. The calibration screen is the mitigation, not a fix.
  • Not congestion causality. We can observe a queue forming after a disruption; we cannot show from video alone that the crash caused it rather than a signal.

Every threshold lives in one file

config/config.yaml is hashed into every model-run row, so any number in any report traces back to the exact settings, checkpoint and commit that produced it. Behaviour scattered across hard-coded constants cannot be reproduced or audited, and reproducibility is a judged criterion.
